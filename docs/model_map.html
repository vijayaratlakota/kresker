Which model does what

VoiceStudio dubbing pipeline, Hindi → Telugu, as running on 17 August 2026. Everything below was read from the running container or measured, not taken from documentation.

End to end

Google Vertex, over the network open-source model, local on the GPU box not a model — rules or signal processing

Every model, in one table

StageModelRuns What it decides, and what it measurably does
1 SeparationDemucslocal, T4 Splits into vocals and background. Everything downstream reads the vocals stem, never the mix.
2 Transcriptiongemini-3.6-flash
via openai-compat-asr		Vertex Owns the words. An LLM doing transcription, so its segmentation and timestamps differ between runs. Its timestamps are not used.
3 Diarizationpyannotelocal, T4 Speaker turns. These decide which cloned voice each line gets, and where a line must be cut.
4 Word timingChirp 2 (chirp_2)Vertex, us-central1 Words and their times in one pass, in 55 s chunks fetched in parallel. $0.016/min. Doubled well-placed lines over the transcriber's own timings (27.9% → 59%+).
4 AlignmentMMS_FA (torchaudio), 315M
+ uroman		local, T4 Given the text, says where each word actually is. This is the accuracy argument: ASR timestamps run ~400 ms, forced aligners under 15 ms. One model covers 1100+ languages; 598 s of audio aligns in ~10 s.
5 Reference gateASTlocal, T4 Answers only “is this a voice talking?”. Used to reject laughter, shouting and crowd noise from the pooled reference. Not applied per line — there, a shout is the point.
6 Translationgemini-3.1-pro-previewVertex The translator. Best meaning of the three tested: 4.54/5 adequacy, 91.7% of lines rated 4-or-5, picked best on 56.9% of lines by a blind judge.
6 Reflectgemini-3.6-flashVertex Critiques and rewrites the draft for natural spoken register. Cannot lengthen a line past its budget — the direct line ships if it tries.
6 Budget repairgemini-3.7-flashVertex Shortens a line that misses its slot. Best compressor measured: 97.2% inside budget against 68.1% for 3.1-pro.
6 Loss checkgemini-3.1-pro-previewVertex Asks whether the shortened line dropped anything. Necessary: ungated, the compressor lost information on 68% of the lines it touched.
7 SpeechOmniVoice TTS, 24 kHzlocal, T4 Clones from reference audio + reference text. Accepts no emotion input — expression comes from the reference clip alone.
8 Cleanup—local Lead-in attenuation, sibilant-burst removal, 32-step floor. Signal processing, no model, and off limits.
9 Fit & mix—local Strict slot, underrun filled by slowing to a 0.85× floor, then mixed over the background stem.
One live discrepancy, from your own log Transcription is calling gemini-3.6-flash. The intent was 3.7-flash, and the proxy was configured with PROXY_ASR_MODEL=gemini-3.7-flash — but the application names the model in each request, so the proxy default never applies. 3.7 measured the best coverage of any word source tested (98.6% against 91–93% for Chirp), so this is worth changing. It is a client-side setting, not a proxy one.

The idea the design turns on

Three different questions, three different models. Conflating them is what produced the faults this project spent its time removing.

Inside stage 6: four models, in order

Deliberately not used

ModelWhy not
Chirp 3Behind an allowlist, and it refuses word_level_confidence — the one feature we want from it. A better transcript with no word times leaves us where the transcriber already is.
CosyVoice 3In the registry, and the only engine here whose instruct takes free-form natural language ("emotion/dialect/speed"). Would be the move if instruct-driven emotion is ever wanted — an engine switch, with its own quality trade-offs.
Claude / AWS BedrockNo Bedrock Claude model accepts audio, and the GPU box has no AWS credentials at all.
Amazon TranscribeMeasured. Best raw words of any source, worst placement after alignment (74.7% well placed against 84.7% for Chirp).
Whisper / WhisperXNo aligner for eight of the target languages (ta kn bn mr gu pa or as). MMS_FA covers 1100+ with one model.
Wav2Lip, any video modelNo lip sync in this pipeline. ElevenLabs does not do it either — their "100% match" means phrase-level isochrony.
