VoiceStudio dubbing pipeline

As implemented and running — verified against the container, 17 August 2026. Hindi → Telugu, ~9 minute videos, wall-to-wall speech. Sections marked OURS are work added on top of the stock application.

Where it runs

VoiceStudio 0.4.2 in Docker on an AWS T4 box, publishing port 3900. Its Python lives inside the container at /app/backend and is patched in place. A separate gemini_proxy.py on the host, port 8900, presents an OpenAI-compatible /v1 API over Google Vertex; both the ASR engine and the LLM engine point at it.

The stages, in order

1

Extract and separate

Audio is taken off the video and Demucs splits it into vocals.wav and no_vocals.wav. The background stem survives into the final mix — that is how the music is kept.

2

Transcribe

asr_backend: openai-compat-asr → the proxy → gemini-3.7-flash. It runs on the vocals stem rather than the mixed audio, in timed chunks streamed to the UI over SSE. Word-level times are kept on the global timeline so the next stage can re-split. Because this is an LLM doing transcription, its segmentation and timestamps are non-deterministic between runs. clean_up_segments then runs.

3

Diarize

pyannote assigns speaker turns, unless the ASR backend returned turns inline. assign_speakers_from_diarization labels each segment and resplit_segments_by_diarization cuts any segment straddling a speaker change.

4

Retime OURS

services/chirp_wire.py, hooked into dub_core.py at the SSE endpoint the UI's button hits and at the legacy CLI endpoint. It runs after diarization and before clone extraction, so the cast, the per-segment reference clips and the voice bindings are all built on the timeline that ships. Inside it, in this order:

  • Chirp 2 returns words with times, in 55 s chunks fetched in parallel, cached per job and keyed on the audio's size and mtime.
  • Partition search. A line is a contiguous run of words, so choosing line boundaries is choosing a partition. Four (pause, max-length, snap) starting points, each one repaired before a winner is picked.
  • Partition repair. Any line forced alignment cannot place gets three candidate moves — merge left, merge right, split at an internal pause — worst line first, and a move is taken only if it strictly improves the weaker side.
  • Re-recognition of weak spans. A line that will not align has bad word times, not a bad window, so that span alone is re-asked. Chirp's times drift inside a long chunk; a short call has no drift to inherit.
  • MMS re-anchoring. Each block's span is replaced by where forced alignment actually finds its words.
  • Repetition collapse. Chirp once emitted one word 90 times over 8 s of shouting, and handing that to the TTS is what made the “aaaahhh” noises. A line is dropped only if its text is degenerate and its audio fails the speech test. DROP_NONDIALOGUE is off: a shouting character is still that character.
  • Gap recovery. Every stretch of speech with no line over it is re-recognised on its own; where Chirp returns nothing, the transcriber's words are used with forced-aligned positions, gated on their own alignment confidence.
  • Sparse-line splitting. A line averaging over 1.2 s per word is not one line; a line under 0.45 s cannot be spoken.
  • Speaker split. Blocks are chosen on acoustic evidence, then cut at speaker changes. Blocks decide timing, parts decide voices — a line carries one speaker_id, so a line holding three people would get one cloned voice for all of it.
  • Resolve and breathe. Overlaps are fixed by trimming the earlier line's end, never by moving a later start, because an early start is the expensive error. breathe then extends ends toward the next onset, capped at 0.30 s.
  • Onset snap. Starts move onto the voiced run they sit in, capped at 0.60 s, never past the previous line's end.

Four gates can reject the whole result and return the transcriber's timing untouched: too few or too many lines, under 60% of the original character count, under 50% of the speaking time, or an unplayable timeline.

5

Clone voices OURS

Per speaker, reference clips are cut from spans where that character is actually talking, clear of neighbouring speakers. Four candidate assemblies are built (clean and properly-spoken → clean → above-gate → best-available) and whichever measures best as an assembled reference wins — scoring individual slices backfired, because three short “good” clips concatenated read as non-speech at the joins. The speech gate is a hard reject, not a demotion.

6

Translate

Two branches, chosen by the provider picked in the UI. The LLM branch batches 20 lines per call across a 12-thread pool, with gemini-3.1-pro-preview for the direct pass and gemini-3.6-flash for the reflect pass; length_broker.py sets and enforces a per-line character budget calibrated against the transcript it is given. The Argos branch is offline and skips the broker entirely.

7

Render

Per-line TTS in the cloned voice, with voice_binding guaranteeing each segment maps to the right one. Under strict slot the engine is asked for the whole slot as its target; underruns are filled by slowing the audio toward its slot with a floor at 0.85×; trailing silence is trimmed. Then the clean-audio work — lead-in attenuation, sibilant-burst removal, the 32-step floor — which is off limits and verified at 5 markers after every change.

8

Mux

Dubbed lines over no_vocals.wav, total duration unchanged.

What is not in the pipeline

  • No lip sync. ElevenLabs does not do it either; “100% match” in their marketing means phrase-level isochrony.
  • No video intelligence. The speaker-change information it would give us already comes from pyannote, which runs on the audio and is more precise about turn boundaries.
  • No Chirp 3. It is behind an allowlist and refuses word_level_confidence, which is the only feature we want from it.
  • MODE=lines is built but off. Transcriber's words, forced-aligned times, no Chirp at all. It lost 7 points of placement for a whisker of coverage at twice the wall clock.

Revert levers: MODE=wire bash _revert_today.sh undoes the 17 August timing work and keeps the hook; MODE=coverage undoes only the gap work; MODE=all covers timing, cloning, render and proxy. Nothing in any lever deletes a job, an audio file or a transcript.
